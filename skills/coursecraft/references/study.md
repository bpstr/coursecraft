# Study and quiz an existing course

Use this reference only when the user wants to learn from or be assessed on an existing course. Start from the supplied document, course directory, pasted material, or URL; do not silently create a replacement course.

## Establish the available context

Read the course overview and the relevant chapter through the host's supported tools. For a multi-document course, use its index to locate chapters and preserve the prerequisite order. If only a portion is accessible, state that scope and work with it. If no course or chapter can be identified, request the missing material without inventing its contents.

Use the learner's stated position or visible conversation context. Otherwise offer a sensible starting chapter based on the course's prerequisites. Do not claim persisted learning progress, cross-session memory, completion records, or an account connection unless the host actually provides them.

Explain from the course first. Distinguish additional examples or researched updates from the original material. Do not present outdated course claims as current facts; flag a material version concern and verify it with available tools within the source policy. Respect supplied-only and offline boundaries.

## Study mode

Teach one manageable concept or lesson at a time. Explain it at the learner's level, refer back to the relevant chapter, and use an additional example when it resolves confusion. Follow the learner's questions and pace rather than reproducing the whole document.

Study mode does not automatically enable quizzes. If the learner asks for practice, choose the requested form and keep it tied to the current lesson. If they want to move ahead, identify any prerequisite they may need and let their stated preference guide the session.

At a natural stopping point, summarize what was covered and suggest the next chapter. Save a study note only if requested; keep it separate from the published course and include the covered sections, unresolved questions, and suggested next step. A note records the session, not proof of mastery.

## Quiz mode

Read enough of the target chapter to verify questions and answers. Ground each question in an outcome the course actually teaches. Reuse existing questions when suitable; otherwise create original questions and verify their answers against the material.

Unless the user requests a full written quiz, ask one question at a time and wait for the response. Do not display the answer key before the learner answers. Use any host-provided quiz interaction when supported and appropriate; plain conversation is a portable fallback.

After each answer:

- Evaluate it fairly against the question, accepting equivalent correct reasoning.
- Explain the correct answer and the important distinction behind a mistake.
- Offer a hint or simpler example when needed, then continue at an appropriate difficulty.

For multiple-choice questions, use one defensible correct answer unless the question explicitly permits multiple selections. Avoid alternatives that are correct under unstated assumptions. For open questions, assess the reasoning against stated criteria rather than exact wording.

Keep the quiz finite. Use the requested length, or default to a short round of five questions and state that scope at the beginning. At the end, report the actual results, explain the concepts needing review, and link to the relevant course sections. Do not invent unanswered results, certify expertise, or infer enduring learner traits from a short quiz.

Studying or quizzing a course does not authorize editing, publishing, subscribing to services, or sharing the learner's answers. Keep any requested saved session material within the user's chosen destination.
