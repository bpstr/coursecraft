# Harbor handoffs: ownership, status, and evidence — outline

**Goal:** Given a Harbor task record, maintain accurate owner and status fields, write an actionable handoff, and document whether acceptance evidence supports Done.\
**Audience:** Engineers who are new to Harbor.\
**Starting knowledge:** Familiarity with engineering tasks, drafts, and reviews; no Harbor experience. Acceptance conditions and evidence are explained in the course.\
**Scope:** Ownership, watchers, explicit status changes, handoff notes, and completion checks from the supplied handbook.\
**Estimated time:** About 18 minutes, within the requested 15–20 minutes; includes reading and practice, and is an estimate rather than a measured completion time.\
**Language:** English.\
**Practice:** Exactly one practical exercise per chapter, three total. No quizzes or reflection questions. Exercises produce task records and notes on paper or in a text editor.

## Recurring example

The handbook's task is to publish an onboarding page. Lea has a draft; the acceptance conditions require a reviewed page and a working navigation link. Omar is away, and the navigation link has not been checked. The team agrees that Mina will collect the review and verify the link. Mina becomes owner, and the task is Waiting until the required review can happen.

The course labels earlier and later practice states as illustrative extensions. They add no Harbor product behavior. The course includes local, clearly simulated task comments for learners to link in their notes; these are practice inputs, not observed review or navigation results.
